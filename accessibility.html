Access to health care

How many people live near DGHS-listed hospitals, primary care and blood banks, and where that access is unequal, from division to union.

How to read: values are shares of people unless you switch the map to land, and distances are straight-line kilometres, not travel time. Each union's population is assumed to be spread evenly across the union. Facility counts are registry listings, not service quality or capacity.

People versus land

The same distances measured two ways: the share of people (solid lines) and the share of land (dashed lines) within each straight-line distance of the nearest facility. People live closer to towns and markets than land does, so the people curves sit higher; the Sundarbans, rivers and hill forests hold land but few people.

Inequality between people

Lorenz curves: areas are ordered by facilities per person; each curve shows what share of all facilities serves what share of all people. The diagonal is an equal distribution. Gini 0 = equal, 1 = all in one place. Q5/Q1 = mean rate of the best-served 20% of people ÷ the worst-served 20%.

Spatial clusters

Moran's I (upazilas; neighbours share a border): above 0 means similar values sit next to each other. Local indicators (LISA) mark high–high and low–low clusters. 999 random permutations, p < 0.05, no correction for multiple tests, so treat clusters as exploratory.

Primary-care structure

Government planning: about one community clinic per 6,000 rural people, and one union-level centre in every union. "Union residents" here means people living outside paurashavas and city corporations (slightly more than BBS's "rural" definition).
